# Relais -- quantified FMEDA (mission M39)

> **Standing.** Relais is a demonstrator *aligned with* annex 6 of UN Regulation No. 79. It is **not** compliant, not approved and not certifiable. This document certifies nothing: it turns the failure modes of the safety case into numbers, so that the safety argument can be attacked with numbers.

Generated by `host/fmeda/fmeda.py`; do not edit by hand. The failure modes are the ones of the generated FMEA (`host/safety/fmea.py`, read-only); the diagnostic mechanisms are the ones that exist in the code, each one quoted with the file that implements it.

## 1. Scope and safety goal

**Safety goal** (from `docs/SAFETY.md` section 1): the actuator must never stay without a fresh command for longer than a bounded, measured interval, and it must never apply the command of a machine that has been deposed.

**In the model** (random hardware failures): the two QNX computers, the actuator arbitration controller, the actuator power path and mechanics, the two machine-actuator links and the inter-computer link.

**Out of the model, on purpose** (and therefore *not* covered by any number of this document):

- systematic faults and software defects of the two copies of the same binary (DEG-08): the model counts the *actuation* of such a defect, never the probability of having written it;
- the timing of the emulated target: every latency of the bench is measured under emulation (SAFETY.md section 7.5);
- the level-X extensions: a third machine to vote (REP-08), two actuator channels (ACT-10), a dissimilar backup (DEG-08). They are listed because they are the way out of the conclusion of section 5, not because they are counted;
- the security threat model beyond frame authentication (SAFETY.md section 7.6).

## 2. Method and criteria

Each row of the FMEDA is one failure mode of one element. The rate of the row is

```text
lambda(row) = lambda(element) * share(mode) * instances(element)
```

and it is split into the four classes of ISO 26262-5, Table 4:

| Class | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `SD` | safe failure, detected |
| `SU` | safe failure, undetected (dormant, or latent) |
| `DD` | dangerous failure, detected |
| `DU` | dangerous failure, undetected |

The diagnostic coverage `DC` of a mode is the probability that the mechanism detects the failure. The class values are **cited**: ISO 26262-5:2018, Annex D, Table D.2 -- diagnostic coverage targets: low 60 %, medium 90 %, high 99 %. The *assignment* of a mechanism to a class is engineering judgement, and it is listed in the table of section 4.

The derived metrics are:

```text
DC    = (SD + DD) / (SD + SU + DD + DU)
SFF   = (SD + DD) / (SD + DD + SU)
SPF   = DU / (SD + SU + DD + DU)
SPFM  = 1 - SPF
LFM   = 1 - SU / (SD + SU + DD + DU)
PMHF  = DU + residual   (the double faults that still reach
                           the dangerous state)
```

The residual multiple-fault term is **computed, not neglected**: the double faults of the two computers are lambda^2 times a window -- the FTTI (8 ticks = 40 ms) when both die, the repair time (1 h, **HYPOTHESIS**: no repair data is available offline) when the second fault strikes while the redundancy is down. It weighs 4.00e-03 FIT, that is 7.55e-05 % of the total, so it changes nothing in the conclusion.

The criteria are **cited**:

- ISO 26262-2:2018, Table 4 -- hardware architectural metric targets (SPFM: 60/90/97/99 %, LFM: 60/60/90/90 % for A/B/C/D);
- PMHF targets, quoted with their edition so that the reader checks the copy in hand: `ISO 26262-2:2018 Table 4` (A < 1e5, B < 1e6, C < 1e7, D < 1e8 FIT) and `ISO 26262-4:2009 Table 4` (B < 1e3, C < 1e2, D < 1e1 FIT). The conclusion of this document does not depend on that choice: the criterion that decides is SPFM, whose targets are the same in both editions.

### 4.1 The rows that carry the conclusion

- **ECU / F8 -- machine computes a wrong value, a certified shadow exists** (180.0 FIT, 1.8 FIT of dangerous-undetected). Dangerous for the value applied, but detected and alarmed. Mechanism: order/shadow comparison over three ticks raises the disagreement alert (DET-09, REP-05). Implementation: `core/src/arbiter.c:241 (REP-05/DET-09), core/src/arbiter.c:293 (three-tick streak)`.
- **ECU / F8 -- machine computes a wrong value, no certified shadow** (20.0 FIT, 20.0 FIT of dangerous-undetected). The one dangerous-undetected share of the software: a third machine (vote) is the level-X answer, REP-08. Mechanism: none: the comparison needs a certified second; without it the wrong value is applied silently. Implementation: `core/src/arbiter.c:265 (comparison needs the shadow)`.
- **ECU / F9 -- second fault strikes before the redundancy is restored** (0.0 FIT, 0.0 FIT of dangerous-undetected). Dangerous for the goal (no fresh command) but detected: the safe state is reached and announced. Mechanism: the actuator counts p+1 ticks without a valid order, plays the will up to its horizon and then falls back to its own damped loop: red lamp and stop requested (ACT-02, ACT-03, DEG-05). Implementation: `core/src/control.c:165 (I6, the horizon of the will), core/src/arbiter.c:231 (the fallback loop)`.
- **ECU / F10 -- both machines dead** (0.0 FIT, 0.0 FIT of dangerous-undetected). Dangerous for the goal, detected: the safe state is reached. Mechanism: same path: the will covers the horizon, then the local damped loop holds the rack (ACT-02, DEG-05). Implementation: `core/src/control.c:165 (I6, the horizon of the will), core/src/arbiter.c:231 (the fallback loop)`.
- **ACT-CTRL / F11 -- arbitration core stops (crash, deadlock)** (150.0 FIT, 150.0 FIT of dangerous-undetected). Single point of failure: nothing observes the arbiter but itself. Mechanism: none: the actuator is a single channel, no second arbitration path at the base level (ACT-10 is the level-X extension). Implementation: `core/src/arbiter.c (one arbiter), host/safety/fta.py (F11 out of scope)`.
- **ACT-CTRL / F11 -- arbitration core computes a wrong mandate or value** (100.0 FIT, 100.0 FIT of dangerous-undetected). Systematic/software defect: excluded from the random-failure budget by ISO 26262, but counted here because the honest answer is that nothing detects it at runtime. Mechanism: none at runtime: the invariants are checked offline (explorer, verifier), not on the target. Implementation: `core/src/invariants.c (offline checks), host/verify/relais-verify.c`.
- **ACT-CTRL / F11 -- position feedback path faulty (sensor and wiring on the target; simulated plant in the demonstrator)** (100.0 FIT, 100.0 FIT of dangerous-undetected). In the demonstrator the rack is the software plant model (core/include/relais/control.h:85); on a real target this is a sensor. Mechanism: none: the law trusts the measurement, no plausibility check compares the rack with the command (CMD-06 bounds the command, not the response). Implementation: `core/src/control.c:65 (error = setpoint - pos), core/src/control.c:72 (clamp of the command)`.
- **ACT-CTRL / F11 -- power or clock transient leaves the arbitration running with a corrupted state** (50.0 FIT, 50.0 FIT of dangerous-undetected). Dangerous and silent: a watchdog is the cheapest lift. Mechanism: none: no watchdog, no self-test of the arbitration state at the base level. Implementation: `core/src/arbiter.c (no supervisory loop around the arbiter)`.
- **ACT-PWR / F11 -- power stage or motor failure: no torque is delivered** (750.0 FIT, 750.0 FIT of dangerous-undetected). Dominant term of the whole FMEDA: see the sensitivity analysis. Mechanism: none: the arbiter sees a command, never the reaction of the rack. Implementation: `core/src/arbiter.c:192 (the applied value), core/include/relais/control.h:85 (the plant is simulated)`.
- **ACT-PWR / F11 -- mechanical jam or excessive friction** (450.0 FIT, 450.0 FIT of dangerous-undetected). Same blind spot as the drive failure. Mechanism: none: no current, speed or position plausibility check. Implementation: `core/src/control.c (no deviation check between command and rack)`.
- **ACT-PWR / F11 -- short or insulation fault in the power path** (300.0 FIT, 300.0 FIT of dangerous-undetected). Would also break the actuation channel itself. Mechanism: none: the fault is invisible to the arbitration. Implementation: `core/src/arbiter.c (no supervision of the power path)`.

Two rows of that table deserve a comment, because they look counter-intuitive:

- `all lambda xN` leaves every ratio untouched and multiplies the PMHF by N: the conclusion is a statement about ratios, not about the absolute scale of the hypotheses (the double-fault rows scale with the square of the rates, but they weigh a few milli-FIT, so the PMHF follows the linear scale);

This is a property of the metric, not of the design, and it is why the PMHF criterion exists next to the SPFM.

## 7. What must be measured on the real target

Every rate of section 3 is a hypothesis. These are the measurements that would replace them, in the order that pays:

1. **Actuation chain failure rate.** Run the actuator on the real power stage (motor, drive, mechanics, cabling) and count the failures per hour; then repeat with a current, speed and position plausibility check, to measure the coverage that check buys.
2. **Actuator arbitration controller.** Same, with the watchdog and the self-test that would replace the `none` coverage of the modes of section 4.1.
3. **Computers.** Failure rate of the QNX computer over a long campaign, split into the modes of section 4 (death, freeze, slowness, wrong value, latent).
4. **Links.** Failure rate per link (connector, transceiver, cable), and the measured detection time of each mechanism (p+1 ticks, bail_ticks, the two-tick ETAT watchdog) under the real timing, not under emulation.
5. **Diagnostic coverage.** Measure the coverage of each mechanism by fault injection on the target: how many corrupted frames does the CRC-32C / SipHash MAC really catch, how many wrong values does the order/shadow comparison really catch when the shadow is not certified, how many silent links does the bail timer really catch. This is the only way to replace the `high` / `medium` classes by measured numbers.
6. **Timing.** The FTTI of the will (8 ticks = 40 ms) and the p+1 detection interval must be measured on the target, with their distribution, because the whole residual analysis rests on the FTTI being short.

## 8. Limits

1. The rates are hypotheses, not measurements (section 3); the sensitivity analysis of section 6 bounds them.
2. The DC classes are cited targets, the assignment is judgement (section 2).
3. Systematic faults, common to the two copies of the binary, are outside the random-failure model (DEG-08); the model counts their *actuation*, not their probability.
4. The PMHF target quoted by edition may differ from the copy in hand; the conclusion does not depend on it, because SPFM decides.
5. The residual double-fault term is computed with a repair time of 1 h (a hypothesis); it is negligible, but it grows with the repair time and would matter if the machines were left unattended for weeks.
6. A diagnostic coverage is never invented here: a mechanism that exists in the code is credited with the class of ISO 26262-5 Annex D that its behaviour justifies, and a mechanism that does not exist is credited with nothing.
